The Balancer 2

The Balancer's big brother: a steel ball balanced on a plate that tilts in every direction, thanks to three motors working as a team.

How does it work?

The same idea as the Balancer 1… but in every direction!

Expert level

This is the hardest robot in the workshop. Build the Balancer 1 first: it teaches you PID with just one direction. Here we do the same, but twice at once!

Hi!

Remember the Balancer? The ball could only roll left or right. Here, the ball can go off in every direction! So the plate must tilt forward, backward, left, right… all at the same time. To do that, you need three motors working as a team.

The eyes

The touchscreen

Two sheets that conduct electricity, one on top of the other. Where the ball presses, they touch: the Arduino measures where, left to right and top to bottom.

The brain

The Arduino

The same one as the Balancer 1! But now it runs two PIDs: one for the length of the plate, one for the width.

The muscles

The 3 servo motors

MG996Rs, much stronger than the Balancer 1's. Each one lifts or lowers one corner of the plate.

The legs

Arms, links and ball joints

Each motor turns an arm. The arm pushes a link, and the link pushes the plate through a ball joint.

The screen feels the ball → the brain runs 2 PIDs → the 3 motors tilt the plate… 50 times a second!

Let me explain

How do three motors tilt a plate? First the brain decides how much to tilt, lengthwise and widthwise. Then for each corner it works out how far it must go up or down. Finally, with a triangle (the arm, the link, and the distance between them), it finds each arm's angle. That's geometry: the maths is already written in the program!

Why a steel ball?

The touchscreen feels pressure. A ping-pong ball weighs barely 3 grams: it wouldn't even notice it! A 30 mm steel ball weighs over 100 grams, and that it feels very well.

Play with the two brains

The brake that stops the ball circling around.

With so little D, the ball goes round and never calms down. Raise Kd little by little… And try Kd = 0 to see!

These are the same numbers as in the big program. Circles A, B and C show whether each corner goes up (green) or down (orange). Click the plate to put the ball down.

Print the parts

6 to 8 hours of printing · the printer works on its own

Download the parts and programs

  1. Open the ZIP file. Inside: three parts to print (.stl) and the three programs (.ino).
  2. Print biellettes-x3.stl first: it's small and quick. Slide an M3 nut into the slot at the end to check it fits.
  3. Then support-plateau.stl and socle.stl, flat, with no supports. The base is 17.6 cm wide: it fits on an 18 cm printer bed.
  4. Suggested settings: 0.2 mm layers, 3 perimeters, 25 % infill.
Tip

While the printer works, play with the simulator above. Once you can tune Kp and Kd on screen, the real robot will be much easier!

2

Get the links ready

15 minutes
3 links3 M3 nuts3 ball studs
  1. Slide an M3 nut into the slot at the end of each link, lying flat.
  2. Screw a ball stud into the small hole at the end: its thread goes through the nut. Hand-tight, don't force it.
  3. The other end of the link, with the big round hole, will be the elbow.
Stuck?

If the nut won't go in, the hole is a bit tight on your printer. Ask a grown-up to press it in with a warm soldering iron, or use a small file.

3

The motors in the base

30 minutes
Base3 MG996R servos12 M3 × 12 screws + nutsScrewdriver
  1. Slide each servo into the window of a wall from the front: shaft at the top, facing out of the wall.
  2. All three servos must be mounted exactly the same way. Turn the base round: every wall should look identical.
  3. Fix each servo with 4 M3 screws and their nuts behind the wall.
  4. Put a label on each servo: A, B and C, going anticlockwise when you look from above.
What do you think?

Why three motors, and not two or four? Hint: a three-legged stool never wobbles, even on an uneven floor.

The wiring

30 minutes · power supply unplugged!

The motors take their energy from the 5 V power supply, not from the Arduino. The two long lines along the edge of the breadboard are the energy roads: + and −.

PartWireGoes to…
Servo A, B, CorangeArduino pins 9, 10 and 11
Servo A, B, Cred+ line of the breadboard
Servo A, B, Cbrown− line of the breadboard
5 V power supply+ / −+ and − lines of the breadboard

The magic wire

The wire between the Arduino's GND and the − line is the one people forget most… and without it, the motors can't understand the orders! Everyone must share the same −.

Which wire is X, which is Y?

With a grown-up, set the multimeter to Ω. Between wires 1 and 3 it should find a few hundred ohms, and the same between 2 and 4. If it finds something between 1 and 2 instead, swap wires 2 and 3 on the Arduino.

Golden rule

Wire everything with the power supply unplugged. The capacitor has a direction: the white stripe always goes on −. Have a grown-up check everything before plugging back in.

5

Arms in the middle

20 minutes · on the computer
  1. Plug in the 5 V supply, then the Arduino over USB. Upload this program:
test_motors.ino
// Testing the plate's three muscles
#include <Servo.h>

const int PIN_SERVO[3] = {9, 10, 11};   // servos A, B, C
bool JUST_CENTRE = true;     // true = the arms go to the middle and stay still

Servo servo[3];

void move(int angle) {                 // angle: 0 = middle, + = one way, - = the other
  for (int i = 0; i < 3; i++) servo[i].writeMicroseconds(1500 + angle * 11.1);
}

void setup() {
  for (int i = 0; i < 3; i++) servo[i].attach(PIN_SERVO[i]);
  move(0);                             // 1500 microseconds = the middle
}

void loop() {
  if (JUST_CENTRE) return;             // stay still
  move(20);   delay(1500);             // all three arms one way…
  move(0);    delay(1500);
  move(-20);  delay(1500);             // …then the other
  move(0);    delay(1500);
}
  1. The three motors move a little, then stop: they are right in the middle.
  2. Push an aluminium arm onto each shaft, perfectly level and pointing outwards from the base. Tighten its little screw.
  3. Change JUST_CENTRE to false and upload. Watch the first movement: all three arms should go up together.
Going down?

No problem! Write it down: in the big program you'll set DIRECTION = -1. Since the three motors are mounted the same way, they always all do the same thing.

6

The legs

25 minutes
3 links3 M3 × 16 screws3 lock nuts6 washersRuler
  1. Pick the hole on the aluminium arm that is about 25 mm from the shaft. Measure the exact distance, from the centre of the shaft to the centre of the hole, and write it down: that's ARM.
  2. Fix the elbow: M3 × 16 screw through the arm and the link's big hole, a washer on each side, then the lock nut. The link goes on the outer side, ball at the top.
  3. Tighten just enough so nothing wobbles, but the link still swings freely.
  4. Measure from the centre of the elbow to the centre of the ball: aim for 70 mm. Screw the ball in or out so all three links are exactly the same length. Write it down: that's LINK.
Careful

If an elbow is too tight, the motor strains and gets hot. If it catches, loosen the nut a little quarter turn.

7

The plate

30 minutes
Plate holder3 ball sockets3 M3 × 10 screwsTouchscreenDouble-sided tape
  1. Ball sockets first: put an M3 × 10 screw through each slot of the holder from the top (the head hides in the recess), and screw a ball socket onto it underneath.
  2. Then the screen: stick it onto the holder with double-sided tape, nicely centred. Its long side must point towards servo A, and its ribbon cable should come out towards the Arduino.
  3. Lay the plate on the three links and press firmly on each ball joint: click! The sockets snap onto the balls.
  4. Look from the side: each link should be straight up. If one leans, loosen its screw and slide the ball joint along the slot.
Did you know?

This three-legged mechanism is called a parallel robot. You find them in flight simulators, in robots that sort biscuits at top speed, and even in some 3D printers!

Calibrating the screen

20 minutes · on the computer
  1. Upload this program, then open Tools → Serial Monitor and pick 115200 baud.
test_screen.ino
// Testing the plate's eyes: the touchscreen
// The 4 touchscreen wires, in order: X+, Y+, X-, Y-
const int XP = A0;   // X+
const int YP = A1;   // Y+
const int XM = A2;   // X-
const int YM = A3;   // Y-

void setup() {
  Serial.begin(115200);
}

void loop() {
  // Is anything pressing on it?
  pinMode(YP, INPUT); pinMode(XM, INPUT);
  pinMode(YM, OUTPUT); digitalWrite(YM, LOW);
  pinMode(XP, INPUT_PULLUP);
  delayMicroseconds(200);
  bool pressed = (digitalRead(XP) == LOW);
  pinMode(XP, INPUT); pinMode(YM, INPUT);
  if (!pressed) {
    Serial.println("Nothing on the screen");
    delay(200);
    return;
  }
  // Measure X
  pinMode(XP, OUTPUT); digitalWrite(XP, HIGH);
  pinMode(XM, OUTPUT); digitalWrite(XM, LOW);
  delayMicroseconds(200);
  int rawX = analogRead(YP);
  pinMode(XP, INPUT); pinMode(XM, INPUT);
  // Measure Y
  pinMode(YP, OUTPUT); digitalWrite(YP, HIGH);
  pinMode(YM, OUTPUT); digitalWrite(YM, LOW);
  delayMicroseconds(200);
  int rawY = analogRead(XM);
  pinMode(YP, INPUT); pinMode(YM, INPUT);

  Serial.print("X = "); Serial.print(rawX);
  Serial.print("   Y = "); Serial.println(rawY);
  delay(200);
}
  1. Put the ball right by the short edge on servo A's side: write down X, that's X_MAX. Then by the opposite short edge: that's X_MIN.
  2. Same on the two long edges for Y_MIN and Y_MAX (Y_MAX on servo B's side).
X doesn't change?

If Y changes when you move the ball along the length, X and Y are swapped. With the power unplugged, swap wires A0 ↔ A1 and A2 ↔ A3.

9

The Balancer 2's big program

20 minutes

Here's the whole brain. Copy your measurements (ARM, LINK) and your calibration into the top of the program, then upload it and put the ball in the middle.

plate.ino
// ===========================================================
//  The Balancer 2 · The Plate — the big program
//  The eyes: the touchscreen. The muscles: 3 servo motors.
// ===========================================================
#include <Servo.h>

// ----- The wiring -----
// The 4 touchscreen wires, in order: X+, Y+, X-, Y-
const int XP = A0;   // X+
const int YP = A1;   // Y+
const int XM = A2;   // X-
const int YM = A3;   // Y-
// Servo motors A, B, C (anticlockwise, seen from above)
const int PIN_SERVO[3] = {9, 10, 11};

// ----- Your calibration (step 8) -----
int X_MIN = 100;  int X_MAX = 900;   // raw values at the two short edges of the screen
int Y_MIN = 150;  int Y_MAX = 870;   // raw values at the two long edges
const float WIDTH  = 154.0;           // size of the touch area, in mm
const float HEIGHT = 86.0;

// ----- Your measurements (step 6) -----
float ARM  = 25.0;   // from the servo shaft to the elbow hole, in mm
float LINK = 70.0;   // from the elbow hole to the centre of the ball joint, in mm

// ----- Lili's settings -----
int   DIRECTION = 1;            // set to -1 if the arms go down when they should go up
int   DIRECTION_X = 1;          // set to -1 if the ball runs away along the length
int   DIRECTION_Y = 1;          // set to -1 if the ball runs away along the width
int   TRIM[3] = {0, 0, 0};      // small corrections so the plate is perfectly flat
float Kp = 0.12;   // P: Push      (degrees of tilt per mm away)
float Ki = 0.02;   // I: Insist
float Kd = 0.05;   // D: Dampen
const float MAX_TILT = 10.0;    // the plate never tilts more than 10 degrees
bool  CIRCLE = false;           // true = the ball goes round in a circle!

// ----- The robot's geometry (don't change it, it matches the 3D parts) -----
const float R_SERVO = 55.0;     // distance from the centre to the servo shafts
const float R_JOINT = 80.0;     // distance from the centre to the ball joints under the plate
const float LEG_ANGLE[3] = {0, 120, 240};

Servo servo[3];
float x = 0, y = 0;             // where the ball is (mm, 0 = in the middle)
float xBefore = 0, yBefore = 0;
float vx = 0, vy = 0;           // its speed (mm per second)
float sumX = 0, sumY = 0;
float restHeight;               // height of the ball joints when the plate is flat
unsigned long timeBefore = 0;
int   missing = 0;              // how many turns since the screen last felt the ball

// Reads the screen. Returns true if the ball is on it, and puts its position in x and y.
bool readBall(float &bx, float &by) {
  // 1. Is anything pressing on it?
  pinMode(YP, INPUT); pinMode(XM, INPUT);
  pinMode(YM, OUTPUT); digitalWrite(YM, LOW);
  pinMode(XP, INPUT_PULLUP);
  delayMicroseconds(200);
  bool pressed = (digitalRead(XP) == LOW);
  pinMode(XP, INPUT); pinMode(YM, INPUT);
  if (!pressed) return false;

  // 2. Measure X: current flows from X+ to X-, we listen on Y+
  pinMode(XP, OUTPUT); digitalWrite(XP, HIGH);
  pinMode(XM, OUTPUT); digitalWrite(XM, LOW);
  delayMicroseconds(200);
  int rawX = (analogRead(YP) + analogRead(YP)) / 2;
  pinMode(XP, INPUT); pinMode(XM, INPUT);

  // 3. Measure Y: current flows from Y+ to Y-, we listen on X-
  pinMode(YP, OUTPUT); digitalWrite(YP, HIGH);
  pinMode(YM, OUTPUT); digitalWrite(YM, LOW);
  delayMicroseconds(200);
  int rawY = (analogRead(XM) + analogRead(XM)) / 2;
  pinMode(YP, INPUT); pinMode(YM, INPUT);

  // 4. Turn the raw values into millimetres
  bx = (rawX - X_MIN) * WIDTH / (X_MAX - X_MIN) - WIDTH / 2;
  by = (rawY - Y_MIN) * HEIGHT / (Y_MAX - Y_MIN) - HEIGHT / 2;
  return true;
}

// Height of a ball joint -> angle of the arm (the triangle trick: arm, link, and the distance between them).
float armAngle(float height) {
  float dx = R_JOINT - R_SERVO;                  // horizontal gap between shaft and ball joint
  float d  = sqrt(dx * dx + height * height);    // distance shaft -> ball joint
  float c  = (ARM * ARM + d * d - LINK * LINK) / (2 * ARM * d);
  c = constrain(c, -1.0, 1.0);
  return degrees(atan2(height, dx) - acos(c));   // 0 = arm horizontal
}

// Tilts the plate: tiltX and tiltY in degrees.
void tilt(float tiltX, float tiltY) {
  for (int i = 0; i < 3; i++) {
    float a  = radians(LEG_ANGLE[i]);
    float rx = R_JOINT * cos(a);
    float ry = R_JOINT * sin(a);
    // how far this ball joint must go up or down
    float dz = rx * tan(radians(tiltX)) + ry * tan(radians(tiltY));
    float angle = armAngle(restHeight + dz);
    servo[i].writeMicroseconds(1500 + DIRECTION * angle * 11.1 + TRIM[i]);
  }
}

void setup() {
  Serial.begin(115200);
  float dx = R_JOINT - R_SERVO - ARM;
  restHeight = sqrt(LINK * LINK - dx * dx);
  for (int i = 0; i < 3; i++) servo[i].attach(PIN_SERVO[i]);
  tilt(0, 0);                    // start nice and flat
  delay(1000);
  timeBefore = millis();
}

void loop() {
  unsigned long now = millis();
  if (now - timeBefore < 20) return;            // 50 times a second
  float dt = (now - timeBefore) / 1000.0;
  timeBefore = now;

  // 1. The eyes look
  float nx, ny;
  if (!readBall(nx, ny)) {
    missing++;
    if (missing > 10) {                          // no more ball: go back to flat
      tilt(0, 0);
      sumX = sumY = 0; vx = vy = 0;
    }
    return;
  }
  if (missing > 10) { x = xBefore = nx; y = yBefore = ny; }  // the ball is back: start again from zero
  missing = 0;
  x = 0.6 * x + 0.4 * nx;                        // smooth out jumpy readings
  y = 0.6 * y + 0.4 * ny;

  // 2. The brain calculates
  float targetX = 0, targetY = 0;
  if (CIRCLE) {                                  // the target goes round the plate
    float t = now / 1000.0;
    targetX = 35 * cos(t * 1.5);
    targetY = 20 * sin(t * 1.5);
  }
  vx = 0.7 * vx + 0.3 * (x - xBefore) / dt;
  vy = 0.7 * vy + 0.3 * (y - yBefore) / dt;
  xBefore = x; yBefore = y;
  float ex = x - targetX, ey = y - targetY;
  sumX = constrain(sumX + ex * dt, -100, 100);
  sumY = constrain(sumY + ey * dt, -100, 100);

  float tiltX = DIRECTION_X * (Kp * ex + Ki * sumX + Kd * vx);
  float tiltY = DIRECTION_Y * (Kp * ey + Ki * sumY + Kd * vy);
  tiltX = constrain(tiltX, -MAX_TILT, MAX_TILT);
  tiltY = constrain(tiltY, -MAX_TILT, MAX_TILT);

  // 3. The muscles move
  tilt(tiltX, tiltY);

  // For the Serial Plotter: the ball and the target
  Serial.print(x); Serial.print(" "); Serial.print(y); Serial.print(" ");
  Serial.print(targetX); Serial.print(" "); Serial.println(targetY);
}
  1. With no ball, the plate should stay flat. If it leans, correct it with TRIM: a few tens (for example 20 or −20) for the motor of the corner that's too high or too low.
  2. Press gently with your finger near corner A: corner A should go up, to send the ball back to the middle. If it goes down, set DIRECTION_X = -1.
  3. Do the same near corner B to check DIRECTION_Y.
The ball runs away?

That's normal the first few times! One setting at a time: first the DIRECTION values, then the PID numbers in the next step.

10

Tuning, like a real engineer

As long as it takes!

Open Tools → Serial Plotter: you see four lines, the ball's X and Y position, and the target. Change one number at a time, upload, watch.

1. Just P

Set Ki = 0 and Kd = 0. Raise Kp until the ball circles around the middle without falling off.

2. Add D

Raise Kd little by little (0.02, 0.04…). The ball calms down. Too much D? The motors shake.

3. A pinch of I

If the ball always stops a little away from the middle, raise Ki very gently.

The big challenge

Once your ball holds steady in the middle, set CIRCLE = true and upload. The target starts going round… and the ball follows it all around the plate!

The Arduino keeps restarting?

The motors are asking for too much energy at once. Check the capacitor and the magic wire, and use a 4 A supply if yours is 3 A.
