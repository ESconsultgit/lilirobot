How does it work?

A sign that knows when you arrive is already a little robot!

Hi!

This time, no need to print letters: the panel is made of 256 tiny lights. By switching on the right ones it can draw any word… and slide it along like the news ticker at a train station. And when someone comes to your door, it wakes up to say hello!

And I'm Livia!

I'm Ellie's little sister. Since I'm smaller, I ask lots of questions… and sometimes I make mistakes! When you see a pink bubble, that's me talking. And do you know why the workshop is called LILI? ElLIe + LIvia = LILI!

The eyes

The motion sensor

It feels the warmth of your body when you move in front of it. Its white dome is a lens that looks in every direction.

The brain

The Arduino Nano

It keeps your messages in memory and decides when to wake up, what to write, and when to go back to sleep.

The mouth

The LED panel

256 lights in 32 columns and 8 rows. A little chip, the MAX7219, switches them on very fast, row after row.

The body

The case

A box with a window for the panel and a round hole for the sensor. 3D-print it or build it from wood.

The sensor feels someone → the brain wakes the panel → the messages scroll… then everything sleeps when nobody's left.

Let me explain

How can a letter move? In fact, nothing moves! The panel draws the letter, then draws it again one column further left, again and again, dozens of times a second. Your eyes think it's sliding. It's the same trick as a cartoon!

Get your messages ready

10 minutes · at the top of the page
  1. Go back up to the message workshop and type your name: the welcome message fills itself in.
  2. Add your other messages, one per line: a joke, “Knock before you come in”, your bedroom weather…
  3. Click Someone's coming! to watch your panel wake up and scroll everything.
Tip

Ellie remembers your messages in this browser. You can come back and change them any time: the program in step 5 updates itself.

2

The case

2 to 3 hours of printing, or 1 hour of woodwork with a grown-up

Option A · 3D-printed

Download the case and the programs

  1. Print boitier.stl lying on its front (the window against the bed), with no supports. It's 17.2 cm wide.
  2. Print couvercle.stl flat, rim facing up.
  3. Suggested settings: 0.2 mm layers, 3 perimeters, 15 % infill.

Option B · Wooden

  1. Cut two boards of plywood, 180 × 80 mm: the front and the back.
  2. On the front, mark the window: 127 × 31 mm, centred, 12 mm from the top edge. With a grown-up: drill a hole in each corner, then cut it out with the jigsaw.
  3. Drill the sensor hole (24 mm bit), centred, 24 mm from the bottom edge.
  4. Cut the 10 × 35 mm strip: 2 pieces of 180 mm (top and bottom) and 2 of 60 mm (sides). Glue them as a frame behind the front.
  5. Cut a 12 mm notch in the bottom piece for the USB cable. The back will screw on with 4 small screws, so you can open it again.
Why is the window a bit small?

It's 1 mm smaller than the panel. That way the panel rests behind it and can't fall through… but every dot stays visible!

The wiring

20 minutes · USB cable unplugged!

No breadboard this time: each wire goes straight from one pin to another. The panel has pins on both ends: the Arduino goes on the end marked DIN.

Ellie's trick

The Arduino Nano has only one 5V pin. But the panel passes the energy through to its other end! So the sensor takes its VCC and GND from the panel's output pins.

The two orange dials

On the sensor, turn the “Time” dial all the way left (the program keeps time itself), and leave the “Sensitivity” dial in the middle. If there's a little yellow jumper, put it on H.

4

The first test

15 minutes · on the computer
  1. In Arduino IDE, open Tools → Manage Libraries, search for MD_Parola and click Install. Agree to install MD_MAX72XX too.
  2. Pick the Arduino Nano board, plug in the USB cable, and upload this little program:
test_panel.ino
// Test: does the LED sign work?
#include <MD_Parola.h>
#include <MD_MAX72xx.h>
#include <SPI.h>

#define HARDWARE_TYPE MD_MAX72XX::FC16_HW  // jumbled letters? try GENERIC_HW
MD_Parola sign = MD_Parola(HARDWARE_TYPE, 10, 4);   // CS on D10, 4 modules

void setup() {
  sign.begin();
  sign.setIntensity(2);          // soft light for the test
  sign.displayText("Hello!", PA_CENTER, 50, 1000, PA_SCROLL_LEFT, PA_SCROLL_LEFT);
}

void loop() {
  if (sign.displayAnimate()) sign.displayReset();   // start again, forever
}
  1. “Hello!” should scroll from right to left.
Won't upload?

Lots of “compatible” Nanos want a different setting: Tools → Processor → ATmega328P (Old Bootloader), then try again.

Jumbled or backwards letters?

There are several kinds of panels. Replace FC16_HW with GENERIC_HW (or ICSTATION_HW, or PAROLA_HW) until it reads properly. Write down the one that works!

5

Your sign's big program

10 minutes

This program was written for you, with your messages from the workshop. If you had to change FC16_HW in step 4, change it here too. The green comments explain each part.

welcome_sign.ino
  1. Upload it. At first the panel stays dark: that's normal, nobody has come by!
  2. Wait one minute: the sensor needs to wake up too when it's switched on.
  3. Wave your hand in front of the white dome… Hello!
It switches on by itself?

The sensor feels warmth: a radiator or the sun can fool it. Point it somewhere else, or turn the “Sensitivity” dial down a little.

6

Close the case and hang your sign

20 minutes
  1. Check everything works before you glue!
  2. Put the panel behind the window, lights facing forward, with the DIN end on the same side as the Arduino. With a grown-up, put 4 dots of hot glue on the edges of the board.
  3. Slide the sensor dome into the round hole, and glue its board the same way.
  4. Stick the Arduino to the bottom with double-sided tape, its USB socket facing the notch.
  5. Close the lid, put two screws in the wall 10 cm apart, and hang your sign at face height, near the door.
Where to hang it?

The sensor sees up to about 5 metres. Point it to where people come in, not at your bed, or it'll say hello every time you turn over at night!
